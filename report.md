# Annual Report 2025: Northwind Technologies

Prepared by the Office of Strategy and Operations. This report summarises the company's performance, projects and plans for the 2025 financial year. It is a fictional document written for practice with retrieval-augmented generation.

## Executive Summary

Northwind Technologies ended 2025 with revenue growth of 14 percent and a stable operating margin of 18 percent. The company expanded into two new markets, completed its cloud migration ahead of schedule, and grew its workforce from 410 to 465 employees.

Three themes shaped the year: reliability of the core platform, faster delivery of customer features, and disciplined cost control. Each department contributed to these goals, and their results are described in the sections that follow.

The leadership team is confident about 2026. Customer demand remains strong, the product pipeline is full, and the balance sheet allows continued investment in people and infrastructure.

## Methodology

The figures in this report come from three sources: the company's financial system, the engineering project tracker, and a survey of department heads completed in November 2025. Financial figures are audited. Operational figures, such as deployment counts and ticket volumes, are taken directly from system logs.

Survey responses were collected from 14 department heads, with a response rate of 100 percent. Where survey data and system data disagreed, system data was used. All percentages are rounded to the nearest whole number, and all monetary amounts are in Swiss francs unless stated otherwise.

Year-over-year comparisons use the 2024 financial year as the baseline. Forward-looking statements in the Outlook section are estimates and are not guarantees of future results.

## Section 1: Finance

The finance department closed the year with total revenue of CHF 182 million, up 14 percent from CHF 160 million in 2024. Operating costs rose by 11 percent, which was slower than revenue growth and lifted the operating margin from 17 percent to 18 percent.

Cost control was a priority. The department renegotiated three major vendor contracts, saving CHF 2.4 million per year, and introduced a monthly spending review for every department. Travel spending fell by 22 percent after the company adopted a stricter approval policy.

Cash and short-term investments stood at CHF 47 million at year end. The department paid down CHF 10 million of long-term debt and set aside CHF 6 million for a planned data centre exit. The annual audit was completed with no material findings.

## Section 2: Software Engineering

The software engineering department had a strong year. The team of 120 engineers shipped 9 major releases and more than 1,400 production deployments, compared with 5 major releases and 780 deployments in 2024. Median deployment time fell from 52 minutes to 21 minutes after the build system was rewritten.

The largest project was the migration of the core platform from two on-premises data centres to a public cloud provider. The migration finished in October, six weeks ahead of schedule, with zero customer-facing downtime during the cutover. Infrastructure cost per active customer fell by 19 percent.

Reliability improved as well. Platform availability reached 99.97 percent, up from 99.91 percent, and the number of severity-one incidents dropped from 17 to 6. The department introduced blameless post-incident reviews and an on-call rotation that limits any engineer to one week in six.

The team also launched an internal developer platform, built an AI-assisted code review tool now used by 85 percent of engineers, and began a pilot of a customer-facing assistant for the product documentation. Priorities for 2026 are security hardening, reducing build times further, and expanding the assistant pilot.

## Section 3: Human Resources

Human resources supported company growth from 410 to 465 employees, hiring 96 people while 41 left, which is an annual attrition rate of 9 percent. Time to hire for engineering roles fell from 62 days to 44 days after the interview process was simplified.

The department launched a new onboarding programme that pairs every new hire with a mentor for their first 90 days. In the November survey, 91 percent of new hires rated onboarding as good or excellent, up from 68 percent.

Learning and development spending rose to CHF 1,900 per employee. More than 300 employees completed at least one internal course, and 58 employees were promoted. The company also introduced a flexible work policy that allows up to three remote days per week for most roles.

## Section 4: Marketing and Sales

Marketing and sales opened offices in Vienna and Milan, the company's first two entries into new markets. The two offices contributed CHF 9 million in revenue within their first nine months, ahead of the CHF 6 million target.

Marketing generated 31,000 qualified leads, up 24 percent, while cost per lead fell by 12 percent. The largest channels were partner referrals, webinars and the annual customer conference, which drew 2,400 attendees.

The sales team closed 38 new enterprise contracts and renewed 94 percent of existing enterprise customers. Average contract value rose by 8 percent, mainly because more customers adopted the analytics module.

## Section 5: Customer Support

Customer support handled 61,000 tickets during the year, a 6 percent increase that was well below the growth in customers. Median first response time improved from 3.1 hours to 1.4 hours, and 89 percent of tickets were resolved within one business day.

Customer satisfaction scored 4.5 out of 5. The most common complaint was the difficulty of finding answers in documentation, which led to the documentation assistant pilot with the software engineering department.

The department introduced a follow-the-sun support schedule across Zurich, Vienna and a partner centre in Lisbon, giving customers coverage for 20 hours per day on weekdays.

## Section 6: Legal and Compliance

The legal and compliance department completed the company's certification against an international information security standard and updated all customer data processing agreements to reflect new European data protection guidance.

The team reviewed 410 contracts during the year, reducing the average review time from 9 days to 5 days with standard templates. There were no regulatory fines and no reportable data breaches.

In 2026 the department will focus on preparing for new European regulation of artificial intelligence systems, including an inventory of AI features and a risk classification process for each one.

## Outlook for 2026

Northwind expects revenue growth of 12 to 15 percent in 2026, with the operating margin held at 18 percent or higher. Planned investments include security hardening of the platform, expansion of the Vienna and Milan offices, and the continued rollout of AI-assisted tools for engineering and support.

The company plans to grow headcount by about 10 percent, concentrated in engineering, sales engineering and customer support. Risks include currency movements, competition for technical talent, and the cost of compliance with new regulation.
